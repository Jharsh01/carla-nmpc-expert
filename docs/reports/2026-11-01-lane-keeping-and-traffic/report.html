Lane keeping and traffic: what the runs taught

simlingo_nmpc --no-cot --fast · Town12 · 31 Oct – 1 Nov 2026 · branch lane-band-worker-timing

Where things stand

The car now holds the lane's centre and only takes stops from SimLingo, unless SimLingo's line leaves the lane by more than 1 m. Route 0 finished all 15 runs, and the weave is largely gone. Routes 1, 8 and 10 still don't finish, with no collisions: the car waits in steady traffic until the 70 s watchdog ends the route. Getting through that traffic is the next job, and a design for it is ready but not built.

What we learned

The wobble came from the path, not from SimLingo

SimLingo's line is steady in the world: each new prediction lands 6–8 cm from the last. The old path was forced through the car, so a few centimetres off the line became a sharp bend just ahead of the bumper. Following the lane's centre removes that bend in normal driving. The same kink is still there while following SimLingo through a turn or lane change.

Lane change or turn can be read from SimLingo against the map

The check compares SimLingo's line, from 8 m ahead, with the map lane. Over 1 m off starts following SimLingo, and under 0.5 m ends it. The largest offset in lane keeping was 0.57 m and real manoeuvres showed 2.6–2.7 m, so the thresholds have room. Measuring against the lane means a bend in the road doesn't count as a manoeuvre.

Lane lookups need a height

Town12's roads are 298–380 m up. Asking the map at height 0 gave the wrong lane on 19 % of road points off junctions, against 4 % at road height. On route 8 that put the car's lane band on the oncoming lane. Fixing it took the route from 10 % to 43 %.

Repeat runs are mostly the same run

Thirteen runs after the height fix, on seeds 1–6, gave only 3 different drives: ten of 30.55 s, two of 28.05 s and one of 27.65 s. They match up to 12 s, and the seed made no difference. A pass count from repeats therefore says little, and what picks the drive isn't known.

The behaviour layer is what holds the car in traffic

SimLingo asks to stop on only 17–28 % of plans on these routes. Routes 8 and 10 have a stream of cars about 2 s apart, and each crossing car's window is widened by 1 s on both sides, so no gap is ever left. On route 8, 145 of 145 plans also had a window that never ends. That traffic runs down the exit lane, so it's traffic to merge with, but it gets classified as crossing traffic because the direction is only read at t = 0.

The gaps on route 8 are tight but usable

The stream runs at 13 m/s with about 1.9 s between cars. By the gap model, merging needs 2.8 s at 3 m/s² and 2.1 s at 5 m/s², so it doesn't fit comfortably, and a to-scale profile clears by 1.3 m ahead and 0.9 m behind. Plain SimLingo did enter this stream, reaching 100 % with no infractions in both runs. Those runs covered 78.6 m in 11.5 s, which looks over-counted.

Route 0 has false stops

Hard stops at 7.1 s and 26.4 s come with cut-in notes reading s 0.0 m, while the real cut-in events are near 12 s and 18.5 s. A car more than 15 m behind seems to project off the lane and read as a cut-in. No test has confirmed this yet.

A log field was misread

The last field of each actor in nmpc.jsonl is metres predicted over 5 s, not a speed. Read as km/h it misled the analysis for a while. A value of 65 means 13 m/s.

The design for traffic

The NMPC has fixed rows, switched by parameters, and rebuilding the solver takes over a minute. So constraints shouldn't be added to the solver at run time. The design keeps the solver fixed and changes what feeds it: each actor becomes a conflict (cross, merge, follow or none), each conflict moves through a lifecycle, and one space-time plan per cycle is compiled into the fixed slots. Nothing here is implemented.

Decision needed before the merge is built

Route 8's slot leaves about 1 m at each end, and the stream won't brake for the car. The minimum margin and how long to wait before accepting a smaller one are safety calls for you to make. The first step doesn't depend on them: fix the classification so stream cars are merges and cars behind are ignored, then test on routes 0 and 8.
