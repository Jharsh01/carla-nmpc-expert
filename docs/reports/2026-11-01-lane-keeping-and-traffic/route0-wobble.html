The main cause: the path is forced through the car

SimLingo's line stays steady on the road. The car weaves around it. simlingo_ref.path_points makes every path pass through the car's own position, so whenever the car is even 5 cm off the line, the path has to bend sharply to rejoin it right in front of the bumper. The NMPC then steers to follow that bend, which moves the car off the line again for the next plan.

The right-hand figures come from re-solving 103 logged plans offline. Each solve used the logged car state with no other cars in the scene, so the car did not actually drive this way. Those replays match the logged steering only moderately (correlation 0.74), so read the size of the drop as approximate. Road curvature in the first 6 m of the path is also 4–5× what it is over the next 12 m (median 0.047 against 0.013 1/m).

Other causes, still unmeasured or partial

  1. Steering jumps at each new plan. 48 % of all steering movement happens on the 25 % of ticks that start a new plan. The biggest jumps (95th percentile) are 0.125 rad in one tick, while the plan's own 0.6 rad/s limit allows 0.03. The kink causes part of this, since removing it cut these changes by 42 %.
  2. Failed solves are still driven. 5 of 106 plans failed while moving, and the agent uses a failed plan anyway. Some of them fall inside the weaves, at 6.95 s and 7.15 s.
  3. Parked cars and the cut-in car. The worst steering is at 6–8 s and 24–28 s, and the first weave is beside parked cars. About a quarter of the logged steering's variation isn't reproduced by a replay without actors, and they are one possible reason. I haven't separated this out.
  4. Car response. The yaw rate follows steering closely (correlation 0.93, 0.05 s lag) but at 0.77 of what a 2.87 m wheelbase predicts. I assumed that wheelbase here, so this needs checking against the NMPC's model.

Size of the wobble, and limits of this run

In this run the car's heading swings 1.5° RMS and up to 6.2° around its 3-second average, with a half-cycle of about 1.2 s. The earlier stalled run had much stronger steering: 95th-percentile lateral acceleration of 4.4 m/s² against 1.4 m/s² here.
