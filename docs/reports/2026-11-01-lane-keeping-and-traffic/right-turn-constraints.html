Right turn with oncoming traffic: constraints and a dynamic constraint system

urban NMPC · right-hand traffic · tested against Bench2Drive route 8 · 1 Nov 2026 · design only, nothing here is implemented

You don't need a solver that adds and removes rows. The NMPC already has a fixed bank of rows that it switches on and off with parameters. The missing piece is a layer above it that turns all the traffic into one space-time tube per plan, so the NMPC gets at most one upper and one lower limit per step, however many cars there are.

  1. Oncoming traffic is rarely the problem on this turn. In right-hand traffic, oncoming cars going straight never cross your path. The real conflicts are the stream from your left, oncoming cars turning left into your exit, and pedestrians.
  2. The car stalls on route 8 because of how it reads the stream. It treats cars it should merge with as crossers that never clear, so the gate never opens.
  3. The 2 s horizon can't see the answer. From rest the plan reaches 6 m, and the merge point is about 10 m away, so the go-or-wait choice has to be made outside the NMPC.
  4. The gap is usable. Plain SimLingo stopped at the line, then slotted in behind a passing car with no contact. Our margins are what rule that out.

Tiers: 1 contact, 2 law and road, 3 spacing, 4 comfort. When nothing is feasible, the lowest priority gives way first. Today every group except headway shares one slack weight, so a lane edge costs as much as a collision.

The dynamic system

Actors never go to the NMPC directly. Each plan runs these steps, and only the last one touches the solver.

tracks + predictions
   1 ConflictMap    each actor against the path over the whole prediction:
                    Cross / Merge / Follow / None, zone [s_in, s_out], window, priority
   2 Registry       one record per (actor, zone), kept between plans, with a lifecycle
   3 Phase          Approach / Hold / Go / Merge / Exit, decides which kinds may be active
   4 Tube search    orders the conflicts over 8 s, gives cap(t), floor(t), v_ref(t)
   5 Compiler       writes the tube and movers into the fixed parameter slots
   6 NMPC           solves, returns slack per group
   7 Feedback       slack and failures go back to the registry

The tube is what stops the piling-on

Every longitudinal constraint is a limit on where you can be at a given time. Once you've picked your place in the order, only the nearest car ahead and the nearest behind matter. Ten cars become one band, and the NMPC already has one cap and one floor per step.

To scale: red bands are stream cars at 13 m/s, 25 m apart. The line is the ego leaving at 1.2 s, at 5 m/s² and 7.5 m/s through the turn. It clears the car ahead by 1.3 m and the one behind by 0.9 m, so this slot only works with no courtesy margin. Most of the decision lies outside the shaded 2 s horizon.

The search is a small grid over time and distance, 8 s ahead, which should take well under a millisecond per pass. It gives at most two tubes to solve: go now, or wait. That replaces one pass:id candidate per actor, and the NMPC horizon can stay at 2 s, since it follows a tube planned over 8 s.

Rules that keep switching safe

  1. No rows are added at run time. The solver's structure is fixed, and rebuilding takes over a minute. The 34 rows per step (850 across 25 steps) already exist. A row turned off is the constant −1, so the bank costs little, and solve time stays near today's 29 ms median and 38 ms at the 95th percentile on route 8.
  2. Never activate what can't be met. Before a limit goes in, check it against braking: if front + v²/2a_min is past it, that order isn't available, and the search picks another. A limit that appears inside the stopping distance is what causes hard brakes and failed solves.
  3. Tighten over time. A new limit moves in from where the warm start already is, over a few plans, so the solver isn't shocked.
  4. Lock the order past the point of no return. Once you can't stop before the zone at comfortable braking, the yield can't be re-added. The Go decision only reverses on a real collision threat.
  5. Check the final plan separately. Test the chosen plan against all actors, including those not in slots. A failure means brake at the limit. That covers the 13 of 153 failed plans the agent drives today.
  6. Separate contact from courtesy. Decide on contact limits first, then add spacing as a soft term. Today's 1 s buffer around every window is courtesy treated as a hard limit, and that's what closes the stream.
  7. Let SimLingo propose, and verify it. When SimLingo's speeds say go, test that tube against contact limits, and accept it if it passes. That's the signal we discard now in keep-lane mode unless it asks to stop.

Order of work

  1. Fix classification. Treat stream cars as merges and cars behind as None. Small, and should remove route 0's false brakes. Test: routes 0 and 8.
  2. Run the ConflictMap in shadow mode. Log it next to today's decisions without acting on it, and compare over routes 0, 1, 8 and 10.
  3. Add the tube search and compiler, replacing the pass: candidates, with the one solver rebuild.
  4. Add the phases, the order lock and the final plan check.
  5. Add offline tests beside parked_car_overtake: stream at 1.9 s and 4 s, oncoming left-turner, pedestrian at the exit, queue in the exit, and the left-hand mirror. Then run routes 0–21.

Risks and open questions

  1. A slot with about 1 m each side is close to a collision. The stream won't brake for you, and any contact stops it being constant speed for the rest of the run. Whether to accept that gap is a safety decision, and it needs a limit you're comfortable with.
  2. Waiting has no end today. In a stream with no other gap, some rule must decide when to accept more risk or give up. The 70 s watchdog also ends the route long before a very patient car would get through.
  3. A bad prediction can poison the tube. Three routes show predicted jumps of 3.7–5.6 km in 5 s. Fix those first, or a single bad track could close the tube.
  4. The 8 s search assumes actors keep their predicted motion. Replanning at 5 Hz corrects this, but the order lock limits how much it can.
  5. The tube search time is an estimate. I haven't built it, so neither that figure nor the claim that solve time holds has been measured.
